# First-Time Owner Execution Guide

This guide assumes you have never handled a boundary line adjustment or subdivision before.

## Phase 0 — before you talk to 1007

Prepare these items:

- Government ID.
- Your phone with a scanning app or PDF scan feature.
- Printed / digital same-day cooperation agreement.
- Printed survey-access page.
- Listing-agent notice form.
- Contact information for 2–3 Idaho licensed surveyors.
- A title/escrow company contact.
- A private due-diligence budget cap.
- A private paid-fallback authority amount if you are willing to pay anything at all.
- A notary option if one can reasonably be present.

Do not bring a blank final deed and do not promise a cash amount you have not decided in advance.

## Phase 1 — neighbor conversation

1. Explain the old one-foot record simply.
2. Ask for the front-only gift, subject to survey and City review.
3. If 1007 says yes, immediately follow the `1007_YES_PATH.md` checklist.
4. If 1007 says no to a gift, use `PAID_FALLBACK_NEGOTIATION.md` only if you have authority to discuss money.

## Phase 2 — order the survey

Call 2–3 licensed surveyors and say you need a **boundary/improvement survey for a proposed City of Coeur d’Alene BLA**, not a house design.

Give them the scope in `MASTER_STRATEGY.md`.

Ask each surveyor:

- earliest field date;
- estimated price;
- whether City BLA / Record-of-Survey work is included;
- whether they will provide the preliminary exhibit before full final drafting;
- whether they will coordinate with title and City staff;
- what information they need from you.

Choose the smallest scope that still proves the exact frontage, structures, easements, access, BLA geometry and future split concept.

## Phase 3 — open title on both parcels

Tell the title/escrow company you are evaluating a boundary line adjustment between 1007 and 1011 N B St.

Ask them to identify:

- exact vested owners of each parcel;
- current legal descriptions;
- deeds of trust / mortgages;
- liens and judgments;
- recorded easements;
- CC&Rs or restrictions, if any;
- whether 1007’s lender must partially release the transfer area;
- recording sequence the title company expects for deed + Record of Survey.

Do not assume the owner can convey the strip free of a lender simply because the owner agrees.

## Phase 4 — get the preliminary BLA exhibit

Before paying for every final document, ask the surveyor for a preliminary exhibit showing:

- exact existing 1011 frontage;
- exact proposed 1011 frontage;
- old and proposed common line;
- all nearby 1007 structures and setbacks;
- your 1011 house and its relationship to a possible future split line;
- resulting parcel areas;
- future conceptual 50/50 subdivision line.

This preliminary exhibit is what you use to ask the City the make-or-break questions.

## Phase 5 — get written City direction

Take the survey exhibit to CDA Planning / Engineering.

Ask the exact questions in `MASTER_STRATEGY.md`, especially:

- does the front-only BLA create recognized 100.00-foot frontage?
- can the future southern lot narrow behind the 50-foot frontage segment?
- short plat or formal plat?
- can the existing house remain?
- alley / rear access / utility / frontage-improvement issues?

Ask for the answer in writing or in a form you can preserve in the project file.

## Phase 6 — decide whether to keep spending

### Green
Continue if the City/survey/title package supports the two-lot path and the cost is still modest.

### Yellow
Redesign the BLA if a structure setback, utility or geometry issue can be solved cheaply.

### Red
Stop if the frontage, house, title, lender or City interpretation makes the two-lot strategy expensive or uncertain.

If red, sell the documented R-12 two-dwelling potential instead of forcing the split.

## Phase 7 — file the BLA

If green:

1. Have Idaho counsel/title finalize the binding agreement language.
2. Surveyor completes the City BLA / Record-of-Survey materials.
3. Submit the City BLA and fee.
4. Answer City comments.
5. Obtain any required 1007 lender release.
6. Finalize the exact metes-and-bounds exhibit.
7. Prepare the final warranty deed.
8. Sign with the required acknowledgment/notary.
9. Let title/surveyor/City coordinate recording order.
10. Obtain conformed recorded copies and final title update.

## Phase 8 — do NOT automatically finish the subdivision

Once the BLA/frontage is proven, pause and re-price the property.

Get broker/BPO opinions for:

- 1011 today as one parcel;
- 1011 with proven split feasibility;
- future house-side parcel;
- future vacant parcel.

Then compare the value premium against the cost/time of finishing the short plat.

## Phase 9 — mortgage work

Call your mortgage servicer before choosing which parcel to sell.

Ask for written answers on:

- current payoff amount;
- whether a parcel split is permitted under the loan;
- partial release requirements;
- required principal curtailment if the vacant parcel is sold;
- whether the loan can be recast afterward;
- recast fee/minimum curtailment;
- whether a refinance would instead be required;
- what happens if the house-side parcel sale pays the loan in full.

Do not assume that sending $200k–$300k of lot-sale proceeds to principal automatically lowers the monthly payment.

## Phase 10 — choose the exit

Current ranking if the split is clean:

1. **Sell house-side parcel → pay mortgage off → retain buildable lot.**
2. **Sell whole property with the split/proof package.**
3. **Sell vacant lot first only if the lender proves the remaining housing payment becomes affordable.**

## Phase 11 — tax review before closing

Give your CPA:

- acquisition basis records;
- improvement records;
- rental / short-term-rental history and depreciation records;
- survey and BLA documents;
- proposed allocation of basis between future parcels;
- expected sequence/timing of house and vacant-land sales.

Ask specifically about the principal-residence exclusion, adjacent vacant-land timing rules, gift basis for the small boundary area, and any rental/depreciation consequences.

## Your project folder

Keep one folder with:

- signed neighbor agreement;
- survey access;
- listing-agent notice;
- buyer assumption if used;
- survey quotes and survey;
- title reports;
- lender release/consent;
- City emails/letters;
- BLA application/approval;
- warranty deed;
- Record of Survey;
- assessor update;
- BPOs/appraisal;
- mortgage payoff/recast correspondence;
- tax advice / closing statements.

The goal is a clean evidence package that a buyer can understand without relying on your memory.
