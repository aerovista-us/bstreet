# Bytecast Owner Briefing

This is the source script used by the B Street Owner Exit Command Center. The site currently uses browser-native speech synthesis so the homeowner can listen immediately without requiring a hosted MP3. A produced MP3 can replace the browser narration later without changing the written script.

## 01 · The 1011 big picture

Here is the big picture. You own 1011 North B Street, and the goal is not to turn you into a developer. The goal is to use a small amount of due diligence to prove whether the property has a stronger exit than simply selling it as-is.

Public-record reconstruction strongly suggests your parcel is about ninety-nine feet wide instead of the roughly one hundred feet that would support two normal fifty-foot frontage lots. The missing piece appears to connect to a historical one-foot transfer involving the south neighbor at 1007.

The preferred concept is small: move the shared line about one foot south at B Street for only about thirty feet, then return to the existing line before the 1007 garage area. If the survey and City recognize one hundred feet of frontage, you may have a credible two-lot path. If they do not, you stop spending and fall back to the property's existing R-12 two-dwelling story.

The reason this matters financially is that your current objective is to exit within about a year and get out from under the mortgage without giving away the upside. If the split proves clean, the leading strategy is to sell the house-side parcel, use closing proceeds to pay the mortgage off completely, and retain the vacant buildable parcel.

That could leave you with lower monthly obligations, cash flexibility, and a debt-free land asset.

The immediate issue is timing. 1007 is for sale. So the first mission is to capture cooperation in writing before that property changes hands, then order the survey and title work quickly.

You do not need to build. You need to turn uncertainty into evidence, and then decide which exit pays you best.

## 02 · If 1007 says yes

If 1007 says yes, do not leave with only a handshake.

First, confirm the yes clearly: they are willing to gift the small front boundary area if the survey, City, title, and lender review show that it does not create a problem for their property.

Second, complete and sign the conditional boundary gift and active-sale cooperation agreement. Fill in the names, date, time, and current listing status, and initial the active-sale section.

Third, sign the survey-access authorization so your licensed surveyor can measure the shared line, nearby structures, and the garage area.

If a notary is available, use the optional acknowledgment. The notary helps prove who signed and when, but it does not transfer the land.

Fourth, scan every signed page before anyone leaves and give both sides a copy.

Fifth, notify the 1007 listing agent that same day so a buyer or title company is not surprised later.

Sixth, order rush survey and title work immediately.

Do not sign a blank deed and do not guess at the legal description. The actual warranty deed comes later, after the surveyor creates the legal description, the City approves the boundary line adjustment, title is clear, and any required lender release is obtained.

If 1007 accepts an offer before the boundary adjustment records, move fast: either complete the approved adjustment before closing, or have the incoming buyer formally assume the cooperation process in writing.

## 03 · The money strategy

The money question is not simply which piece can be sold first. The real question is which transaction actually fixes your monthly burden while preserving the most equity.

You are working from an estimated mortgage balance of roughly three hundred to four hundred thousand dollars. If the split is clean and the house-side parcel can sell for enough to satisfy that loan, escrow can pay the mortgage off completely and you can retain the vacant buildable parcel.

That is why house-side first currently leads. You may walk away with some cash, no mortgage tied to the original property, and a land asset you can hold, sell later, or develop when you choose.

Selling the vacant parcel first is still possible, but it has a trap: the lender may require a partial release and apply the sale proceeds to principal while leaving your scheduled monthly payment largely unchanged unless the loan can be recast or refinanced.

So do not assume a large principal payment automatically solves affordability. Before choosing that route, get the lender's partial-release and recast terms in writing.

There is also a strong middle option: prove the frontage and subdivision feasibility, then sell the whole property with that certainty packaged for a developer or buyer. That may capture much of the upside without forcing you to finish every entitlement.

The rule is simple: do not spend development money just because development is possible. Spend only enough to create marketable certainty, then compare real broker price opinions for each exit.

Your best move is the one that solves the mortgage and leaves the most usable value in your hands.
