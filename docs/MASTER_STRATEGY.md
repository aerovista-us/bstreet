## Why the front-only 1 ft × ~30 ft adjustment is the preferred concept

The proposed boundary line adjustment moves the common boundary about **1 foot south at B Street**, continues west for about **30 feet**, and then returns north to the existing boundary **before the 1007 garage/setback area**.

Why it is better than taking the full one-foot strip for the entire depth:

- It targets the actual bottleneck: street frontage.
- It transfers only about **30 square feet** instead of roughly 117.5 square feet.
- It leaves the current line near the 1007 garage, reducing the risk of worsening a tight garage setback.
- It can be designed with no more than the City’s allowed deflection count for the BLA, subject to survey and City review.
- It appears to leave 1007 with far more than the normal 50-foot R-12 frontage requirement.
- It does not appear to take a conventional two-lot right away from 1007 because even the reconstructed 75–76 feet is below the ~100 feet normally needed for two 50-foot-frontage lots.

## R-12 rules that matter

Working current standards used in the analysis:

- Detached single-family minimum lot area: **5,500 sf per detached SFR lot**.
- Other residential density math: **3,500 sf per unit** except detached-SFR treatment.
- Buildable-lot frontage: normally **50 ft on a public street**, unless an approved alternative or lawful nonconforming condition applies.
- Front yard: **20 ft**.
- Interior side: generally **5 ft**; one side may need **10 ft** if there is no alley or other legal rear access.
- Rear yard: **25 ft**.
- R-12 can support a two-dwelling outcome on one qualifying parcel, including two detached dwellings or duplex housing, subject to all other requirements.

## Hard stop / continue gates

### Continue only if

- Survey + City recognize at least **100.00 ft** of adjusted frontage or give a clear acceptable alternative.
- The proposed BLA creates **no new nonconformity** at 1007 or 1011.
- The 1011 house can coexist with the future split, or the buyer economics justify a redevelopment route.
- Title and any lender partial release are obtainable.
- The due-diligence spend stays inside the owner-approved cap.

### Stop / pivot if

- the frontage still fails and the City does not give a clean alternative;
- the BLA worsens a garage/structure setback;
- title or lender issues make the sliver unreasonably expensive or slow;
- the house blocks the split and the economics do not support redevelopment;
- the owner would have to spend development money instead of simply proving and selling the upside.

If the split path fails, the fallback is to market 1011 as **one R-12 parcel with documented two-dwelling potential**, rather than chase an expensive exception.

## Pre-1960 demolition issue

The existing home dates to 1911. Coeur d’Alene applies a historic-preservation demolition-review process to pre-1960 structures before demolition permitting. This is not necessarily a permanent prohibition, but it adds process and timing. For your current cash-constrained exit, demolition should generally be a future buyer/developer decision.
